# BroTD Infinity — Tiny Swords direction

BroTD Infinity is a portrait Canvas 2D defense prototype with **two levels, each with 20 rounds of 10 waves (400 waves total)**: **Whispering Woods** and **Forgotten Graveyard**. Enemies approach from above; the player recruits swordsmen, archers, healing monks and unlockable lancers on a 5 × 3 deployment grid and protects the king behind them. Pixel Frog's Tiny Swords supplies the allied sprites, ordinary Torch goblins and terrain. The interface uses warm paper, wooden frames and the pack's blue buttons. The separate `king-defense` prototype remains preserved at port 5186.

Start with **125 gold, three starter slaves, three unlocked central tiles, an empty army and a level-1 Treasury**. Convert the starter slaves, arrange the initial formation, then press Start. **Fighters come only from slave conversion; neither recruitment nor personal-level upgrades can be bought for gold.** Additional deployment tiles cost gold. The **Slave Market stays visible to the left of the compact Army grid, above the Barracks**, showing the shared slave balance; tapping it turns **one slave into one individual fighter in the Barracks**. The transaction saves before its short visual sequence: a captive enters Market, the workshop lights up, and the new fighter travels down to the Barracks. Conversion is available immediately. A separate **100-gold passive-income purchase** enables one slave per 30 real minutes, including up to four hours per absence. Changes to the army save immediately, and the next wave uses the latest formation. Current recruitment, personal-level and capture rules are in [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md).

## Campaign and opening balance

The campaign runs from **1-1 through 1-20**, then **2-1 through 2-20**, with ten waves per round. Every tenth wave is a boss encounter: the **Goblin chief / Crypt Spider** in rounds 1–9 and 11–19, replaced by the **Ogre / Crypt King** in rounds 10 and 20. Each level has 18 mini-boss encounters and two main-boss encounters.

**The current curve continues through all 400 waves, preserving waves 1–30 exactly.** From wave 31, total HP rises by 430 per round with small early-wave steps and larger ninth/tenth-wave steps. Enemy counts increase gradually, with at most four arriving together; damage growth tapers toward the personal-level cap. Level 2 continues from Level 1's endpoint using skeletons, skeleton archers and ghouls, instead of restarting a three-times-earlier-forest formula. Its per-kill gold remains doubled by role. See [CAMPAIGN_BALANCE.md](CAMPAIGN_BALANCE.md) for the formula, checkpoints and validation limits.

A starting goblin has **60 HP / 7 damage**, compared with a level-1 swordsman's **60 HP / 6 damage**. The swordsman attacks faster (base interval 1.1 seconds versus 1.45). The first three waves remain unchanged. Wave 1 sends two goblins together and a third six simulation seconds later; wave 2 sends **two goblins and two archers together**. No simultaneous arrival exceeds four enemies.

Arrival times use the **simulation clock**: 0.8 seconds initially, then 14.8 and 28.8 seconds when needed. Wave 1 is the exception, with its third goblin at 6.8 seconds. Groups enter in centered rows, melee in front and archers behind.

Global waves **11–19 and 21–29** each contain **four goblins, two archers and two boars**, entering **4 + 4 at 0.8 and 14.8 seconds**. [opening-curve.mjs](opening-curve.mjs) allocates their increasing HP budget across those roles, using weights 94 / 56 / 104 and correcting integer rounding. Goblin damage is **10 on waves 11–18, 12 on 19–28 and 14 on 29–30**; archer damage is 64% and boar damage 91% of that value, rounded. The first goblin in ordinary waves gets **one extra damage point**, closing a measured post-chief dip for a healer formation. Shared damage steps now fall on the ninth wave, with larger HP increases on ninth/tenth waves and small increases in between. The old rounded linear damage formula created unintended middle-round walls.

The boss at **wave 10** has **two goblins and two archers**: the first arrival contains the chief, two goblins and an archer, followed by one archer (**4 + 1 at 0.8 and 14.8 seconds**). Bosses at **20 and 30** retain **three goblins and two archers**, entering **4 + 2** on the same schedule. The three chiefs' HP is **450 / 688 / 924** and damage **18 / 24 / 31**, respectively. These encounters retain their authored opening budgets. Later bosses now use the continuing campaign budget, with 55% allocated to mini-boss HP or 65% to main-boss HP. Existing attack cadence and animations are unchanged.

Historical opening checks used the actual combat engine through [scripts/combat-balance.mjs](scripts/combat-balance.mjs), with results and limitations recorded in [OPENING_BALANCE.md](OPENING_BALANCE.md). Test armies, personal levels and formations are explicit scenarios, not a simulated natural recruitment journey, an economy forecast or a guaranteed victory curve. The current continuation is checked through static wave invariants, short healer-mechanic tests and renderer checks; complete battles and progression runs are left to the user's playtesting. Recruitment probabilities, 5% stat growth and merging are unchanged.

Separately, [scripts/early-campaign.mjs](scripts/early-campaign.mjs) links real combat to the actual resource APIs and a stated management policy. **Historical snapshot before the wave-10 goblin removal:** six seed/speed scenarios completed thirty waves in **452 battles without timeouts**; one needed 102 attempts rather than the initial 100-attempt limit. In that snapshot, **90 of 138 defeats (65.2%)** occurred on ninth/tenth waves, versus 41.7% before the closing-wave adjustment, with a majority in each round. These results have not been rerun for the five-enemy wave 10 and are not current validation. Fixed management paths are not a population success rate or a guarantee that other waves never cause repeats.

The former **1,680 → 1,054 HP** drop after round 1-3 is removed: waves 30→31 now have **1,680 → 1,695 total HP**. Round 1-5 wave 7 has **2,197 HP**, rather than the previous 1,259. The Level 1→2 boundary carries **8,990 → 9,005 HP**. These are definition totals, not measured difficulty or completion guarantees; composition, reinforcements and army growth still require playtesting.

Per-kill rewards remain **1 gold for goblins/archers/healers, 2 for boars and 20 for bosses**, doubled by the corresponding roles in Level 2. First clears remain **10 × wave-in-round**, totaling **550 per round**. The first ten waves contain **58 enemies / 85 kill gold**; wave 10 itself awards **24 kill gold**. The first three rounds contain **214 enemies** and award **315 kill gold + 1,650 first-clear gold**. Current full-definition totals are **1,907 enemies / 2,709 kill gold in Level 1**, **2,790 / 7,630 in Level 2**, and **4,697 enemies / 32,339 gold** for the campaign including its 22,000 first-clear gold. These arithmetic totals exclude retries, sales and building income; they are not a campaign-completion or elapsed-time forecast.

From **1-11 wave 1 through 1-20 wave 10**, one **Goblin healer** replaces an archer in the second arrival, including boss escorts. It heals a wounded non-healer enemy within 95px for a flat **35–48 HP** per cast, depending on campaign progress, with a 2.6-second base interval. It cannot heal itself or other healers. Healthy support stays behind its fighters; alone, it advances and uses weak melee, and the king can counter it at range. Level 2 has no healer variant yet. The supplied red sprite and native healing ring are reused.

The Level 1 chief and ogre retain their authored heavy-strike animations. Level 2 uses the Crypt Spider and Crypt King, each with a supplied 16-frame atlas: idle, walk, side attack and downward attack. Their contact pose follows the existing 70% impact timing; attack cadence, range and movement speed are preserved. Level 1 bosses are drawn at 52/70 logical pixels of body height, and Level 2 bosses at 54/72. All bosses retain ordinary overhead health bars and a small target windup marker. Boars keep the supplied Unity sheet; ghouls retain its combat role. See [Level 2 map and bosses](assets/GRAVEYARD.md).

## Formation and combat

The compact **FPS** label in the right HUD gutter reports rendered frames from real
animation-frame timestamps, after the existing 30-FPS render cap. It averages about
one second and updates roughly three times per second, independently of battle
speed. Backgrounding resets the sample, so hidden time is excluded. The cap can
produce uneven 20–30 FPS pacing; the counter is an observation tool, not evidence
that a spawn or animated tree caused a slowdown. Rendering behavior is unchanged.

**Mobile UI rule:** use small unit icons and readable levels in lists; reveal stats and actions on tap. Avoid oversized cards, repetitive buttons and menus that consume the battlefield. Keep compact controls usable by touch.

The **5 × 3 Army grid is always visible below the battlefield**, without the king or castle. Tap an empty unlocked cell to open the compact picker and place an individual fighter from the Barracks for free. An occupied cell offers replacement from reserve, Merge, movement and removal. Gold-funded recruitment and Upgrade actions are removed. A locked cell shows its current gold cost; unlocking it opens the unit picker for the newly available cell.

Each deployed fighter in the Army grid shows its personal level as a compact white number with a dark outline to the right of its HP bar. The 14px logical label is constrained to fit levels 1–100 within the cell. Level labels are formation-only: neither the main battlefield preview nor combat gets them. Formation units and placement ghosts use a fixed idle frame. Battlefield idle animations run at 65% of their previous frame rate; walk, strike, shot and healing animation timing stays unchanged. Short general notices use the right gutter between recruitment info and Buildings, leaving the formation and battlefield clear without a permanent notice row.

**Move** closes the picker so the grid remains available. Tapping an empty unlocked destination moves the guard; tapping another guard **swaps their positions**. Both retain their class, identity and personal level. Moving, reserve placement and reserve replacement are free; replacement sends the previous occupant to reserve. Removing a guard also returns that fighter to reserve, with **no gold refund**, and leaves the cell unlocked. The last guard cannot be removed while a battle or its result exists; an empty army remains valid during initial preparation, and Start requires at least one guard.

Conversion, merges, Barracks sales, reserve transfers, movement and cell unlocks **save immediately**. Currency transactions use the live gold and slave balances. Each fighter in reserve is a separate owned unit and can be deployed, merged or sold. There is no separate Army button, Army modal, draft reservation or Save army action. Closing a picker does not undo completed changes. Gold, captures, first-clear rewards and building income earned while editing remain available.

Before Barracks II completes, Market conversion rolls **60% swordsman, 25% archer and 15% healer**. Matching recruit requirements grow with the current recruitment level: **5 x current level** additional fighters (5, 10, 15, 20...). For a fresh account, cumulative counts 5 / 15 / 30 / 50 produce the first level-2 / 3 / 4 / 5 fighter directly from Market; recruitment level 100 requires 24,750 of that type. Merging can raise an owned fighter's personal level sooner. The current conversion is counted before assigning the new fighter's level. **Existing fighters keep their personal levels** when later conversions increase recruitment level. HP, damage and healing each gain **5% of their level-one base per level**, reduced from 10% to accompany merging, additively and rounded to whole values: `round(base × (1 + (level − 1) × 0.05))`. Small base values can remain unchanged at a single step (healer healing is 4 / 4 / 4 / 5 at levels 1–4), while HP increases. A swordsman has 60 HP / 6 damage at level 1, 69 / 7 at level 4 and 87 / 9 at level 10. Existing fighters immediately use the new stat formula. Version-1 recruitment saves migrate once to version 2: received counts stay exact, and a separate fixed legacy training credit preserves earned recruitment level and proportional partial progress, rounded down by less than one recruit. Version-2 reloads reuse that credit without awarding it again. Gold, slaves, roster, formation and existing personal levels are retained.

First use of Market is guided by a small **Tap** label and upward arrow inside its base, plus a soft outline pulse. It appears only while the building can convert a slave, with no menu, reward window or placement action in the way. A successful conversion saves `marketHintCompleted` with the fighter and resource cost; the hint does not return after reload or Reset run. Older saves with any recorded conversion are treated as already learned. Reduced-motion users receive a static indicator. The hint adds no layout row and does not intercept taps.

The small **i** at the upper right of the Army dock opens **Recruitment**, showing the currently unlocked class chances (60% / 25% / 15% before Barracks II; 40% / 25% / 15% / 20% after completion), each current recruitment level and how many more fighters of that type are needed for the next level. Opening this panel is free and available even with zero slaves. The locked Lancer is visible from the start; its Barracks upgrade and acceleration buttons spend the displayed gold only when tapped. It uses saved per-type counts, updates after conversions and shows the level-100 cap. There is no persistent last-recruit caption; conversion has a 1,200 ms animation and a short result notice in the right gutter. The resource spend and new fighter save before the animation, so reloads cannot interrupt ownership or grant a duplicate.

The **Barracks** uses a compact five-column icon grid with personal-level labels, up to ten fighters per page. There are no per-fighter action buttons or large cards in this list. Tapping an icon replaces the list with a small detail view in the same menu: portrait, name, level, HP, Attack and, for healers, Healing. Back or Escape restores the same list page. **Recruit** in this detail view closes the menu and highlights army destinations; the fighter remains in reserve until the player chooses a tile. An empty unlocked tile deploys that fighter for free; an occupied tile swaps its current guard back into the Barracks, preserving both fighters' identities and levels. A locked tile first offers its gold unlock price, then places the selected fighter after purchase. **Cancel** or Escape exits placement without losing the fighter or spending resources. This flow also works during battle, with the saved formation applying from the next wave.

The green **+ Merge** in a reserve or deployed fighter's detail view selects that fighter as the source. Tap **another deployed fighter of the same type** in the Army grid to add their personal levels: level 2 + level 3 becomes level 5. The target keeps its identity and cell; the source is consumed only when the merge succeeds. A deployed source leaves its old tile empty and unlocked. Merging is free, awards no sale gold and does not change slave balances, received totals or future recruitment levels. Empty or locked cells, other classes and the source itself are invalid targets. A sum above **level 100** is rejected without losing either fighter or any levels; exactly 100 is allowed. Cancel or Escape leaves both fighters unchanged. The completed merge saves immediately and affects the next wave, preserving the active battle's snapshot; reloading an unfinished selection consumes nothing.

Holding a fighter on the Army grid or a compact Barracks icon for **450 ms** picks it up for drag merging. Moving more than **10 screen pixels before pickup** cancels the hold and leaves native list scrolling available. Short taps still open details. After pickup, legal Army targets are outlined, the hovered target brightens, and the floating portrait follows the finger; a separate movement beyond 10 pixels is required before a drop can commit. Releasing over a legal target uses the same merge transaction as **+ Merge**, which remains available in both detail views. Self, other types, locked/empty cells, overflow and misses cancel without consuming a fighter. A cancelled reserve drag restores the Barracks page and scroll position. Extra fingers, Escape, input cancellation, loss of app focus, scrolling and viewport changes cancel the gesture. The original icon stays connected until the contact ends so live income refreshes cannot lose a touch release. The unit-picker's quick reserve deployment/replacement taps remain unchanged.

**Sell** beside Recruit in the detail view sells that individual reserve fighter for **1 gold**, then returns to the list. The last owned fighter is protected and the disabled action explains why. Sale feedback remains in the Barracks, and refreshes restore keyboard focus safely. Deployed fighters are never sold from this screen. Individual sales do not reduce received totals or future recruitment levels.

The slave resource uses a shared lightweight captive icon in counters, conversion cost and offline rewards. Combat captures and passive production show **+N and the icon directly over Market for 2.8 seconds**, with a stock-counter pulse. Arrivals earned under a menu wait until it closes; arrivals acknowledged through an offline reward receipt wait for **Collect**, and any restored menu must close before the animation runs. These visual notifications never award resources themselves. Market has a warmer card, a larger counter and an available-recruit outline; stock counters abbreviate values of 1,000 or more while accessible labels and menus retain exact values. Reduced-motion users see static arrival and recruit feedback. General notices occupy the right gutter without covering cells, the king or buildings. The visible Army heading, instruction and capacity counter remain removed. Market stands above Barracks on the left; recruitment info is at the upper right and **Buildings** at the lower right. Cancel replaces info while placing, moving or choosing a merge target. There is no bottom toolbar.

The current battle owns a separate snapshot of its fighters. Editing the grid never moves, heals, upgrades or removes fighters in that battle; the **next wave takes the latest saved formation**. Combat and automatic-wave countdowns continue while editing or using the picker, Settings or Buildings. Wave transitions preserve the selected cell and open picker, so the player can continue arranging the army without reopening it.

Swordsmen advance into melee. Archers approach bow range, and monks follow the front line and approach injured guards to heal them. The grid is a deployment layout rather than a movement boundary: survivors hold their ground between groups and return to formation between waves. Stable targets and distance margins reduce movement oscillation; nearby units separate gently. Approach stopping distances account for targets projected onto the army advance boundary, so swordsmen and archers can reach goblin/skeleton archers near the entrance instead of stopping outside attack range. The boundary and land-only routes remain unchanged.

Monks have a **77.5 px heal radius** and approach injured guards to within 57.5 px before casting. A healthy monk holds position before enemies arrive or when a fighter is already nearby. Monks cannot attack, do not heal full-health targets, and may heal themselves. **The king cannot be healed during battle.** The king has **100 HP**, remains stationary at the rear, and is attacked once the army has fallen. Losing the king ends the attempt; defeating every enemy clears the wave.

The king prioritizes his existing melee attack against any enemy within **48 px**: **4 damage**, with a base **1.2-second interval**. With no enemy in melee range, he can counter **goblin archers and skeleton archers only** within **120 px** using a small golden royal projectile. Each projectile deals a fixed **4 damage** in either level, matching the starting goblin archer, on arrival after travel; its base attack interval is **1.8 seconds** and action duration is **0.8 seconds**. These ranged timings use the same **0.85 unit pace** as enemy archers, and the shared battle clock and speed setting also apply. The king does not move to pursue targets or fire at distant melee enemies, chiefs or bosses. The shot reuses the existing king atlas gesture and draws the small bolt on Canvas, without new images or sound.

Combat retains the original **0.85 unit pace** plus the later **0.85 battle-clock multiplier** at ×1: together, unit movement and attack pace are 0.7225 of the original. The battlefield's speed button cycles **×1 → ×2 → ×3 → ×1**, multiplying the shared battle clock for movement, animations, healing, projectiles, spawning and enrage. Menus, foreground income, capture cooldown, HUD refresh and the delay between automatic waves use unscaled foreground time; offline building income also uses real elapsed time. Sound pitch is unchanged. Speed persists across waves within the session, is not saved, and resets to ×1 on reload or a fresh run.

Enemies enrage at **max(75, last scheduled spawn + 45) simulation seconds**, then their damage grows over time to prevent support-only stalemates. A warning appears 15 simulation seconds before enrage. Later rounds can spawn reinforcements through 70.8 seconds, so the timer expands to give the last group at least 45 simulation seconds before enrage; completion still depends on the army and positioning.

## Barracks II and the Lancer

Barracks II becomes available at **Swordsman recruitment level 5**, not a merged fighter's personal level or a boss clear. Fresh accounts need **50 received swordsmen**; existing legacy training credit remains valid. Construction costs **200 gold** and takes **one real hour**, including offline time, independently of battle speed. The existing **i / Recruitment** menu always shows Lancer as the fourth type: the locked row names the required Swordsman level, then offers the Barracks II purchase when eligible. Construction time and the decreasing instant-finish price stay in that row. After completion it shows the Lancer chance and recruitment progress. Barracks itself only manages reserve fighters; there is no separate building-upgrade screen.

Optional instant completion costs **ceil(100 × remaining milliseconds / 3,600,000)**: 100 gold for a full hour, 50 for thirty minutes, one for the last 36 seconds. Completion at the deadline is automatic and free. Repeated starts, completion callbacks and reloads cannot charge twice or recreate a consumed guarantee.

After completion, the next conversion for **one slave guarantees a level-1 Lancer**. This guarantee persists until used. Later odds are **40% swordsman / 25% archer / 15% healer / 20% lancer**; the existing three-type odds apply until completion. Older types retain received totals and training credits; Lancer starts at zero receipts. Normal five-percent stat growth, same-type merging, placement and one-gold sale rules apply.

The Lancer attacks one enemy from behind the front rank: **48 HP, 7 damage, range 75, base attack interval 1.3 seconds**, versus a swordsman's 60 HP, 6 damage, range 38 and 1.1 seconds. No arrow, area attack or block skill is added. Four native palette atlases and portraits add **78,732 bytes**; the Army grid uses a static formation frame. See the [implemented specification](design/LANCER_BARRACKS_CONCEPT.md) and [asset record](assets/lancer/SOURCES.md). King upgrades and skills remain outside this feature.

## Progression, menus and saves

**Auto waves defaults to On**, including pre-update saves that stored Off. The one-time `autoWavesDefaultVersion: 1` migration enables it without changing army, resources or campaign progress. Choosing Off afterward remains saved across reloads; resetting a run preserves this explicit preference. The first battle still starts after initial formation, then automatic waves continue after victories and defeats.

**Auto waves is On by default.** The first wave still waits for the player to arrange the formation and press Start. After each win or defeat, a **two-second foreground countdown** starts the next attempt automatically using the latest saved army. The countdown is not affected by battle speed and continues while the unit picker, Settings or Buildings is open. After a wave, the army and king recover fully. Gold and captures from kills are retained even on defeat. **Defeat retreats by one global wave**, with 1-1 Wave 1 as the minimum: losing 1-1 Wave 6 prepares 1-1 Wave 5; losing 1-2 Wave 1 prepares 1-1 Wave 10; losing 2-1 Wave 1 prepares 1-20 Wave 10. Army composition, ranks, unlocked cells and Treasury survive. Results save advancement or retreat immediately. Clearing wave 10 advances to the next round; clearing 1-20 Wave 10 advances to 2-1 Wave 1; clearing 2-20 Wave 10 starts another two-level cycle at 1-1 Wave 1 with the latest saved army and existing economy. First-clear bonuses cannot be collected again through retreat or replay.

Switch **Auto waves Off** in Settings to retain manual result screens and return to formation between attempts. Level 1 completion prepares Level 2. After both levels are complete, replay restarts at 1-1 Wave 1 with the existing army and economy.

The rounded **Buildings** button opens Treasury, Slave Market and Army space without stopping play. Three cells begin unlocked; subsequent cells cost **25, 50, 100, 175, 275, 400, 550, 750, 1,000, 1,300, 1,650 and 2,100 gold**, totaling **8,375 gold** for all twelve additional cells. The player chooses each location on the grid. Cell unlocks spend gold and save immediately; previously opened cells stay open without another charge or refund. Captures now have 30% chance per eligible kill, a five-kill guarantee and a 30-second foreground cooldown. The first four captures have 50% chance without waits between them, guaranteed within 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 kills; the fourth begins the normal cooldown. Buildings shows the live rate, guarantee and wait. Treasury earns **1–5 gold per real minute**, with upgrades costing **75 / 150 / 300 / 600 gold**. It also credits up to **four hours of income per absence** automatically on return: **240 gold at level 1**, up to **1,200 at level 5**. A persistent Welcome back! window lists offline rewards; **Collect** closes it. Income-rate upgrades do not extend this limit; storage-duration upgrades are planned for later. Full details are in [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md).

The **Slave Market** uses the new compact blue workshop artwork and remains visible to the left of the Army grid, above Barracks, even before passive production is purchased. Its slave count includes starter supply, combat captures and market production; slaves fund fighter conversions, while gold funds cell unlocks. The one-time **100-gold purchase enables passive production**; it is not required to convert slaves. Once enabled, the market produces **one slave every 30 real minutes**, online or offline, with **up to eight slaves per absence** under the same four-hour storage limit. Production progress carries over between sessions; returning credits completed production automatically. The timer begins at purchase, with no income for earlier time. Market income does not count as a combat capture or change capture guarantees, search progress or cooldown. No market production or storage upgrades are implemented yet.

The header has moved onto the side water: a 44px-wide left block shows **level-round (for example 1-12)**, **Wave / 10** and ten 19px-high local nodes in two columns. The right-hand stack contains **Profile**, a compact 22px-high gold counter and speed. Profile uses a 32px circular avatar with a 44px touch area; speed has the same visual/touch sizes. HUD balances of 10,000 or more are abbreviated, while menus and accessible labels retain the exact gold amount. The ten local nodes switch for each round; the tenth identifies its mini-boss or main boss. The title header, normal-combat objective plaque and floating kill counter are removed to keep the map clear. The speed button remains on the map; army management lives in the grid below it. Brief automatic-wave countdown and enrage notices sit below the left progress block. Preparation uses a compact 84 × 44px Start/Retry/Replay button, with the full wave action in its accessible label. Kill statistics remain in manual results; the bottom panel is dedicated to arranging the army.

**Auto waves, Music and Reset run live under Settings inside Profile.** The profile displays the Telegram first name when available, otherwise Commander, and the current level-round/wave. Music has a saved mute toggle and volume slider, defaulting to 5%. Combat and the automatic-wave countdown continue while arranging the army or using the unit picker, Profile or Buildings. Menu headings, decorative building art, reward rows and spacing are compact; close controls retain 44px touch targets around a 32px visible button. The manual Pause/Resume control and Stop/cancel-battle dialog are removed. Reset requires a second tap and is disabled while a battle or its result is active.

Current save key stays **`brotd-infinity:campaign:v2`**, with **`campaignVersion: 3`** inside the record. This update preserves existing fighters and their personal levels, gold, slaves, unlocked cells, Treasury, market production, capture progress and first-clear history. Older saves acquire an empty reserve and zero recruitment counts; existing fighters do not invent past conversions or retroactive levels. The one-time **`starterSupplyGranted`** marker grants three starter slaves only when a fresh or legacy account has no deployed fighters, no reserve fighters and zero slaves. Existing accounts with fighters or slaves keep their balances; recording the marker prevents later reloads or sales from claiming a new grant. Reset run starts again with three starter slaves. Starter supply does not alter capture history or recruitment totals before conversion. The same record saves reserve fighters, per-type received totals, Barracks level/timestamps/first-Lancer guarantee and the Auto waves preference. Missing Barracks data starts at level I; missing Lancer totals start at zero without resetting existing training credits or fighters. `clearedWaves` now counts global progress from 0 through 400; first-clear IDs are global 1–400. The old **`kings-guard:toon:formation:v1`** prototype save remains separate and untouched. Reloading an unfinished battle retains resources and returns to preparation for that wave with the latest saved army, including every completed edit made during combat.

Hiding or closing the app pauses combat, the automatic-wave countdown and capture cooldown. **Treasury and an income-enabled Slave Market accrue offline income**, each capped at four hours per absence at its saved rate. Loading the game, returning to the tab or resuming Telegram credits income automatically, preserves fractional progress and immediately checkpoints the updated balances and timestamps so another resume does not award the same interval twice. Existing saves without a Treasury timestamp establish a baseline on their first load; they receive no invented retroactive income. Saves without a market purchase begin with passive production disabled; the visible market still converts slaves. Once purchased, passive income survives defeats, campaign replay and reloads; Reset run disables production again. No server/database integration is included.

Offline rewards remain visible until **Collect** is pressed. Gold and slaves are credited and checkpointed once, while an unacknowledged `offlineRewards` receipt survives reloads. Collect clears the receipt without duplicating income. The reward window preserves any menu open underneath and restores its interaction afterwards. Empty rewards produce no popup.
